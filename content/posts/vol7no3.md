#### Ode, snap.
*Vol. 7, No. 3* 

<img class="art" src="https://res.cloudinary.com/dwwbmrctk/image/upload/v1792007595/PLv7n3_sjdi6f.png" alt="35mm film">

I’m not a very good photographer. I tried to change that a few times in my life (as recently as last year, in fact) — I spent too much on a camera and got really into Flickr and hung out with (i.e., tweeted at) creative types who owned nice lenses and had at least a dilettantish interest in taking good pictures — but it didn't matter, I don't have a knack for it and I lack the patience and inclination to learn and improve. (More like F-Oh-Just-Stop-Already, amirite?) 

Last summer, I handed my phone to a stranger and had them take a photo of me with family and friends. When I looked at the photo later, there was a woman passing behind us looking at the camera, as though she were with us, but not, like, *with* us. It was just odd, so for the first time in my life I tried out that thingy where you use your phone and your finger to erase a person. It took four seconds and worked great and felt... weird. 

It's not like photography doesn't have a rich tradition of dishonesty, but I am concerned we are now spending too much effort creating moments when all we really wanted to do is capture them. 

So now I'm a "snapshot" guy. There's an app called [Lapse](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/lapse-disposable-camera/id1636699256), and it more or less operates like the iPhone version of a disposable camera — features that do more to mimic the spirit of those cameras than force fit analogue features for shits, kicks and giggles. It's the *good kind* of nostalgia. (Lapse also seems to have a bit of a history going from simple app, to social photo app, to failed social photo app that burned though tens of millions of dollars, and then, incredibly, instead of just shutting down, they reverted back to the original concept. All of that happened before I found it.) 

Opens immediately to take photos, stores them behind a button for me to review later (I like that feature a lot, and this button push is a much better implementation of it than Gudak's *wait for three fucking days while we pretend develop your fake film*), and does a little voodoo to actually look like proper photograph.

Now you're thinking, "the fuck does 'proper' even mean there?" Well, let me tell you: I am 48 years old, which means my sticky years were like 1992 through 1998 or so, so a proper photo looks like it was taken by a Kodak FunSaver — it's grainy, there might be red eye, and everyone looks a little spooked but also like they are having the best day of their life. Also the date should be stamped on there in an unnecessarily digital-ass font. 

(Also, I bought my kids each one of [these](https://www.kodak.com/en/consumer/product/cameras/digital/charmera-keychain-digital-camera/) and they are pretty great if you're in good light.)
